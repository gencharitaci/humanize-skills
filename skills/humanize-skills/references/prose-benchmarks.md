# Prose Benchmarks — Positive Targets

What "good" looks like after a pass. `core-rules.md` says what to remove; this
file says what to leave standing and what to aim for. Read it with the final
check (§6), not at startup — nothing here loads unless the edit is nearly done.

These are targets, not quotas. Hitting every one mechanically produces a new
uniform — the very failure §6 guards against. One or two per piece, varied
across a body of work.

## Sentence-length variance

Human paragraphs mix lengths without pattern: a 4-word punch beside a
28-word meander, then a medium sentence that takes its time. The tell is never
"short sentences" or "long sentences" — it is three in a row of the same
category (`core-rules.md` §0, rules 1–2). When the draft is all medium-length,
break one sentence in half and let another run one clause longer instead of
trimming everything toward the middle.

## Slack sentences

One or two sentences per text at half pressure — an underdeveloped aside, a
flat plain statement, an unresolved "we'll see." Examples of the register (not
templates to copy): "The rest was routine." / "Whether that holds is a
next-quarter question." / "I don't remember it being that loud." If every
sentence performs, none of them land.

## Uneven confidence

Vary the certainty level across the piece the way the evidence varies: one
flat assertion where the fact is solid, one honest hedge where it isn't, one
open question left hanging. Uniform confidence in either direction — everything
hedged or everything certain — reads as machine-shaped.

## Plain verbs and honest qualifiers

Prefer wrote, moved, used, tried, showed, found. Keep "very," "perhaps,"
"tends to" where the claim genuinely needs them; cut only the stacks. Plain
"is/has" beats "serves as" every time. Superlatives survive when true ("the
first," "the only") — deflating a true superlative is falsification, not style.

## Named-over-vague, once per piece minimum

At least one reference a vague draft would have left general: the title, the
person, the date, the number with its unit, the version. What genuinely isn't
known gets flagged as a gap (`SKILL.md` fact-lock), never papered over and
never invented.

## Reader acknowledged, sparingly

Where the genre allows a first- or second-person register, one moment that
admits the writing exists ("skip this section if you already know the basics,"
"I know how this sounds"). A spice, not a base ingredient — twice is a tic.

## Shelf life

Lexical tells decay: models adapt, famous banned words collapse within a year,
new tics arrive with each generation. Treat every banned-vocabulary list in
this engine as a hint with an expiry date and the habits underneath (rhythm,
structure, specificity discipline) as the actual target. When a banned word
stops appearing in real AI output, retire it from the list with a CHANGELOG
note instead of enforcing a dead rule.
